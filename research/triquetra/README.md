# Triquetra Research: Geometry, Signal Coupling, and Electrical Observations

**Research record date:** 2026-11-13  
**Status:** Preliminary research note; observations are researcher-reported and have not been independently replicated.

## Purpose

This page records a proposed geometric configuration and a set of reported electrical, magnetic, and frequency observations so that other people can assess the ideas and design controlled, safe tests. It distinguishes reported observations from proposed explanations and applications.

## 1. Proposed geometry

The proposed structure places a triquetra inside a circular boundary.

The researcher's working hypotheses are:

- The circular boundary may behave as an antenna or coupling element under suitable dimensions, materials, and electromagnetic conditions.
- The triquetra may affect the distribution or coupling of an incident signal.
- The combined geometry may have measurable effects that differ from either element alone.

These are hypotheses. The geometry alone does not establish antenna performance, signal redirection, shielding, or protection. Those properties depend on dimensions, conductive materials, feed/ground configuration, frequency, polarization, and the surrounding environment.

## 2. Reported frequency observation

The researcher reports observing two sine-wave channels:

- Channel 1: 100 Hz
- Channel 2: 101 Hz

The reported frequency separation is 1 Hz. The source, amplitude, phase relationship, channel coupling, instrument, and measurement file have not yet been supplied. Therefore, this note records the report but does not infer a particular physical effect from it.

If two coherent signals at 100 Hz and 101 Hz are present together, their superposition can produce a 1 Hz beat/envelope pattern under suitable conditions. That mathematical possibility does not demonstrate that the triquetra generates, redirects, or disrupts a signal.

**Evidence needed:** original oscilloscope/audio recording or instrument export; instrument make/model; probe and channel settings; signal source; amplitudes; phase; wiring diagram; and whether the observation was made with the structure connected, powered, or isolated.

## 3. Reported experimental observations

The researcher has described experiments involving:

- LED brightness or state changes when different parts of the structure were touched.
- LED changes when a multimeter probe contacted the structure.
- Voltage readings that changed near a plasma ball.
- Magnetic interaction or movement when magnets were brought near the structure.
- Tests using both powered and unpowered configurations.

A prior reported measurement series included approximately 236 mV near an operating plasma ball and approximately 1,125 mV directly above it. The exact meter mode, range, probe arrangement, reference point, frequency response, and repeatability are not available in this record. These values should be treated as preliminary observations, not calibrated field measurements.

The researcher has also described an unpowered configuration. A change in LED behavior in such a setup would require identifying the available energy source and return/coupling path. Possible sources to investigate include ambient electromagnetic fields, capacitive coupling, static charge, and stored energy. Ground is a reference or return path; it is not, by itself, an energy source.

## 4. Superconductivity hypothesis

The researcher has explored whether some observed electrical or magnetic behavior could indicate superconductivity. The observations currently documented here do **not** establish superconductivity.

A credible test would require, at minimum:

- Four-terminal resistance measurements with calibrated instrumentation.
- Resistance as a function of temperature, with repeatable transition behavior.
- Appropriate controls for contact resistance, wiring, and measurement artifacts.
- Independent magnetic characterization, including a test for flux expulsion (Meissner effect) where applicable.
- Replication by an independent laboratory.

A magnet moving near a sample, an LED changing brightness, or a voltage appearing near a plasma source is not sufficient evidence of superconductivity.

## 5. Proposed applications — unverified

### Signal interaction and personal protection

The researcher proposes that a triquetra enclosed by a circle might interact with an external signal, potentially dispersing or redirecting it. A wearable version has been imagined as a possible protective object.

No protective effect has been demonstrated. No one should rely on such an object for safety or substitute it for established protective measures. Any shielding or field-redistribution claim would need controlled measurements of incident and transmitted field strength across a defined frequency range, with appropriate controls and independent replication.

### Signal interference

The researcher proposes investigating whether a particular frequency configuration could affect an external signal. No effective disruption mechanism or practical result has been demonstrated. Testing should be conducted only in a shielded, lawful laboratory setup and must not interfere with communications, medical devices, or public infrastructure.

### Electricity generation

The researcher is interested in whether the structure could contribute to electricity generation. No net energy generation has been established. Any claim would require a complete energy balance: measured input power, measured output power, stored-energy accounting, uncertainty analysis, and a controlled run showing repeatable net output. A passive geometric structure cannot be assumed to generate energy without an identified energy source.

## 6. Suggested independent test sequence

1. Publish exact geometry, dimensions, materials, conductor layout, and photographs.
2. Characterize the circle alone, the triquetra alone, and the combined structure under identical conditions.
3. Use a calibrated signal generator and oscilloscope to test known signals, recording amplitude, phase, frequency, and field strength.
4. Repeat measurements with the structure absent, disconnected, grounded in a defined way, and isolated, changing one condition at a time.
5. Measure any claimed signal attenuation or redistribution with calibrated probes and a controlled test environment.
6. For energy claims, measure all inputs and outputs with suitable instruments and report uncertainty.
7. For superconductivity claims, use controlled temperature-dependent four-terminal resistance and magnetic measurements.
8. Invite independent replication and publish raw data, negative results, and complete apparatus details.

Do not connect a person to unknown conductors, mains wiring, or energized experimental circuits. Do not use the human body as a test ground. Avoid salt-water body-contact experiments.
